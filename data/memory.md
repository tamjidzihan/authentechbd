## 5. Website Goals

Build a modern e-commerce website that helps customers: - Browse
products and categories. - Search for products and filter listings. -
View product photos, specifications, condition, price, availability, and
warranty details. - Understand whether a laptop is new or pre-owned. -
Contact the shop to ask about a product or availability. - Find the
physical shop and contact details. - Trust the business through clear
policies, transparent product information, and consistent branding.

**Important:** The images do not establish whether online checkout,
customer accounts, payment gateway integration, delivery, inventory
management, or an admin dashboard are required. Confirm these scope
decisions before implementation.

## 7. Homepage Design Direction

A suggested layout that respects the supplied brand artwork:

1.  **Announcement strip (optional):** A short, verified offer or
    service message. Do not invent discounts.
2.  **Main header:** Logo, navigation, search, and clearly visible
    contact/cart actions according to scope.
3.  **Hero section:** Strong headline such as "Technology You Can Trust"
    or a client-approved equivalent, short supporting copy, one primary
    action, and a carefully selected laptop/product visual.
4.  **Category cards:** Use clear images and labels for the seven
    categories shown in the banner.
5.  **Featured products:** Consistent product cards with image, name,
    price, condition, stock state, and action.
6.  **Trust strip:** Tested products, competitive prices, warranty
    support, reliable service---subject to client confirmation.
7.  **Promotional section:** Use a purple/orange accent treatment
    without reproducing the crowded banner as the entire page.
8.  **Visit/contact section:** Siraj Plaza address, phone number, and
    confirmed email.
9.  **Footer:** Navigation, contact details, and only those
    policy/social links the client supplies.

The supplied banner is useful as a brand reference, but the website
should use a responsive layout with live text and individual product
assets rather than stretching the full banner across the page.

## 8. UI/UX Requirements

-   Mobile-first and fully responsive across mobile, tablet, and
    desktop.
-   Clear navigation and visible product search.
-   Consistent product card dimensions and image ratios.
-   High-quality, optimized product imagery with meaningful alt text.
-   Readable typography and accessible contrast.
-   Keyboard-accessible navigation, controls, and forms.
-   Visible focus states and appropriately sized mobile tap targets.
-   Helpful loading, empty, error, and unavailable-product states.
-   Filters and sorting should be easy to clear and understand.
-   Use semantic HTML and accessible form labels.
-   Avoid excessive gradients, glow effects, animations, and visual
    clutter.
-   Do not use fake reviews, fake stock counts, fake discounts, or
    invented customer statistics.

## 10. Technical Architecture

**Not specified by the client.** Choose the stack based on project scope
and hosting constraints.

If building a modern React storefront, a reasonable starting point is: -
React + TypeScript - Vite - Tailwind CSS or the project's existing
styling system - React Router for page navigation - Reusable components
for headers, product cards, category cards, filters, and forms - A

A frontend-only catalog can begin with structured local data, but
production inventory, orders, payments, and admin management need an
appropriate backend or commerce platform. Do not assume a particular
backend, database, hosting provider, or payment gateway until agreed
with the client.

## 12. SEO and Performance

-   Give every page a descriptive title and meta description.
-   Use meaningful product/category URLs.
-   Add canonical URLs and sitemap/robots configuration when deploying
    publicly.
-   Use product structured data only when accurate price, availability,
    and product details are available.
-   Optimize and responsively serve images; avoid using a large
    promotional banner as a substitute for page content.
-   Lazy-load below-the-fold images where appropriate.
-   Keep headings semantic and page content crawlable.
-   Provide a useful 404 page.
-   Add analytics only if the client approves and privacy requirements
    are addressed.

## 13. Security and Production Readiness

If the project includes a backend or admin: - Validate input on the
server. - Protect admin routes and enforce authorization on the server,
not only in the UI. - Keep secrets and API keys out of frontend source
code. - Use HTTPS in production. - Add sensible rate limiting and abuse
protection to inquiry forms. - Avoid collecting unnecessary personal
information. - Configure error handling and logging without exposing
sensitive data.

## 14. Questions to Ask the Client

Before finalizing scope, clarify:

1.  Is the site a product catalog with inquiries, or a complete
    e-commerce store with cart and checkout?
2.  Which payment methods are required (for example, cash on delivery,
    bank transfer, or a specific mobile financial service)?
3.  Does the shop deliver nationwide? What are the delivery fees and
    policies?
4.  Will the client provide product photos, names, SKUs, specifications,
    prices, condition, stock, and warranty details?
5.  Are laptops both new and pre-owned? How should used-product
    condition be described?
6.  What are the exact warranty, return, replacement, and service
    policies?
7.  Does the client need customer accounts, order tracking, and an admin
    dashboard?
8.  Who will add and update products after launch?
9.  Are there official social media pages or a preferred WhatsApp
    contact number?
10. Can the client confirm the address, phone number, and email
    displayed in the banner?
11. Does the client have an approved logo source file, brand color
    values, and preferred fonts?
12. What is the domain, hosting environment, budget, and launch
    deadline?
